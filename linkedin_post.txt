I recently completed a personal power-systems portfolio project: a 138/34.5-kV substation load-flow, N-1 contingency, and capacity analysis.

I modeled a two-transformer substation with a normally open 34.5-kV bus tie and evaluated normal operation, loss of either transformer, corrective bus transfer, and future load growth.

A few results from the study:
• Base load: 40.0 MW at approximately 0.95 PF
• Normal transformer loading: 42.8% each
• N-1 loading at base load: 87.24% on the surviving 50-MVA transformer
• +10% growth: 96.36% N-1 loading
• +13.9% growth: 99.94% N-1 loading with about 0.9597 pu bus voltage
• +14% growth: 100.04%, crossing the transformer thermal limit

The modeled N-1 capacity boundary was therefore about 45.6 MW, with transformer thermal capacity becoming limiting before the selected 0.95 pu voltage criterion.

This was a conceptual study focused on load flow and contingency/capacity analysis rather than protection coordination or detailed substation design. It was a useful exercise in turning a one-line concept into a repeatable engineering study and documenting the assumptions, limits, and results clearly.
